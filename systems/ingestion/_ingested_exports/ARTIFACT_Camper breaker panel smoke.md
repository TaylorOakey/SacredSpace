1. **The washing machine motor burned up**

2. **Smoke later came from inside the camper breaker panel, specifically near the washer/dryer circuit breaker**

# ⚡ **Most Likely Causes (in descending probability)**

### **1. The washer motor failure created a massive overcurrent that stressed the breaker**
A dying motor often:
- pulls **locked-rotor current** (6–8x normal)  
- causes wiring to overheat  
- melts insulation  
- weakens the breaker contacts  

If the breaker was slow to trip or partially tripped under load, the **internal contacts may have overheated and burned**, causing the smoke you saw.

Breakers can literally *cook themselves* when their internals get weakened.

---

### **2. Loose connection at the breaker or the neutral bar**
Inside an RV/camper, wiring is often:
- aluminum or mixed metals  
- vibration-prone  
- exposed to temperature swings  

A single loose screw on a hot or neutral conductor can create **resistance heating**, which:
- chars insulation  
- smokes under load  
- melts the breaker lugs  

The fact that the smoke was *localized to the washer/dryer breaker* heavily supports this.

---

### **3. The washing machine’s burned motor sent heat or fault current back through the circuit**
If insulation melted inside the appliance first, you may have:
- a partial short to ground or neutral  
- heat conducting up the wire bundle  
- arcing inside the junction box or panel  

You mentioned smoldering insulation under the floor; this means **the heat event traveled**, which is extremely important.

---

### **4. A shared neutral or ground path overheating**
Common in older RVs:
- multiple high-draw appliances share a neutral  
- a single stressed neutral can overheat  
- smoke appears at the panel connection instead of the appliance  

---

# 🧭 **Your Next Steps (do these in order)**  

## **Step 0: DO NOT POWER THE CIRCUIT BACK ON**  
Not until the following checks are done. Breaker failures can escalate quickly.

---

## **1. Open the panel (with MAIN POWER OFF) and inspect the washer/dryer breaker**
Look for:
- discoloration  
- melted plastic  
- burnt smell  
- a wire that looks darkened or brittle  
- insulation that looks toasted  

If any of these show up:  
👉 the breaker is cooked and **must be replaced** before the washer circuit is used again.

---

## **2. Check the screw terminals for tightness and heat damage**
A loose hot or neutral on that breaker is a prime suspect.

If the copper looks:
- blackened  
- smoky  
- green/ashy  
- brittle  

…that conductor may need to be cut back and re-terminated.

---

## **3. Inspect the wire run between breaker and washer area**  
Since insulation under the floor smoldered, that means:
- the wire could now have **damaged insulation**  
- a partial short may exist  
- heat may have traveled through a conduit or bundle  

A damaged wire becomes a repeat fire hazard.

---

## **4. Replace the washer breaker either way**  
Even if it *looks* okay, once a breaker has smoked:
- the internal contacts are unreliable  
- trip curve becomes unsafe  
- it can fail to protect next time  

A $10–$20 breaker is cheaper than a rebuilt camper.

---

## **5. Do NOT reinstall the bad washing machine**
A motor that died violently is often:
- shorted internally  
- leaking current  
- melting insulation  

Plugging it back in could replicate the event.

---

# 🛑 Signs You Should Call a Pro Immediately
If you see any of these:
- melted bus bar inside panel  
- charring that spreads beyond one breaker  
- repeated smoking after power is restored  
- any melted neutral/ground bars  
- wiring that has fused to the breaker  

These point to potentially deeper faults.
