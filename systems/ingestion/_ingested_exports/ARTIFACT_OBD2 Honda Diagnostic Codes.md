Yes, installing an incorrect or incompatible fuel pump in your 2002 Honda can absolutely cause the kinds of issues you're seeing. Here's why:

---

### 🚨 Key Issues a Mismatched Fuel Pump Can Cause:

1. **Rich Fuel Mixture (System Too Rich Codes like P0172, P0175):**
   - If the fuel pump delivers fuel at too high of a pressure or volume, the engine can receive more fuel than it needs. This overwhelms the ECU’s ability to maintain the correct air/fuel ratio, triggering "system too rich" codes for both banks.

2. 🧯 **O2 Sensor Voltage High:**
   - A rich mixture causes excessive unburnt fuel in the exhaust. This throws off O2 sensor readings and can cause downstream sensors (B1S2, B2S2) to show high voltage or heater-related trouble codes.

3. 🔥 **Cylinder Misfires (P0301–P0305, P0300):**
   - Overfueling can foul spark plugs and lead to weak combustion or total misfires.
   - Wet plugs or carbon buildup are common when the engine runs rich too long.

4. ⚙️ Long-Term Damage:
   - Catalytic converter damage due to excess unburnt fuel
   - Poor fuel economy, rough idle, and hesitation

---

### 🔍 Things to Check:

- Did the replacement fuel pump match OEM pressure specs (typically 40–50 PSI for a 2002 Honda)?
- Is a fuel pressure regulator installed and functioning properly?
- Are injectors leaking or stuck open due to high pressure?
- Did the pump come with a matching fuel pressure relief valve?
