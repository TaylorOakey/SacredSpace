### ⚙ **ASSISTANT** — 2025-11-15 10:29

This little beast is the *spindle shaft center bolt* on your Scag Tiger Cat II, and it’s notorious for being stubborn because:

• It’s torqued extremely tight from factory  
• It often has Loctite  
• The spindle shaft spins freely unless you lock it  

You’re basically fighting a steel merry-go-round.

Here’s the clean, reliable way to remove it without damaging the spindle, pulley, or bearings:

---

## **1. You MUST stop the spindle from turning**
This is the secret sauce. If the spindle can spin, the bolt will never break loose.

You have **three safe options**:

### **Option A – Use the pulley holes**
Up top (on the mower deck), the spindle pulley has several small circular holes in it.

Stick a **large screwdriver, pry bar, or punch** through one of those holes and brace it against the deck frame.  
This will lock the spindle so the bottom bolt can’t rotate.

This is the method mechanics use most.

### **Option B – Strap wrench**
Wrap a heavy-duty strap wrench around the pulley and hold it.

### **Option C – Impact wrench (if available)**
An impact can “shock” the bolt loose without having to hold the shaft.  
*But the pulley still may spin—so locking the spindle is wise even with an impact.*

---

## **2. Use the correct socket**
It looks like a **15/16"** or **24mm** center bolt on the Tiger Cat II spindle.

Use:

• A **6-point socket**  
• A **long breaker bar** or **half-inch impact**

Avoid 12-point sockets—they round edges.

---

## **3. Break the bolt loose**
Once the spindle is locked:

• Turn **counterclockwise**  
• High torque is normal  
• A quick, sharp pull works better than slow pressure  

If it’s still stuck:

### **Penetrating oil**
Spray the bolt head → wait 10–20 minutes.

### **Heat (optional but extremely effective)**
A small propane torch on the bolt head for 15–20 seconds expands the metal and releases the thread-locker.  
Do **not** heat the spindle bearing housing too much.

---

## **4. If the bolt still won’t move**
Give it a few sharp taps with a hammer on the socket extension.  
Not to destroy it, but to vibrate the threads.

---

## **5. Once loose, pull the blade adapter/spacer off**
You’ll see:

• Center bolt  
• Washer  
• Blade holder (adapter)  
• Spindle shaft itself

Keep track of orientation.

---

## **Quick warning**
Do **not** stick anything into the spindle shaft hole to “hold it still.”  
That can mushroom the shaft end or damage threads.

Lock *only* at the pulley.

### ⚙ **ASSISTANT** — 2025-11-15 10:37

If you don’t have a socket big enough for that big ol’ spindle bolt, you’ve still got a path forward — a few, actually. Think of it like reaching for the right spell in your mechanical spellbook.

Here’s how to break it open without the right socket on hand:

### **1. Measure the bolt head with whatever you’ve got**
Even holding another socket up next to it will give a rough guess.  
On most Scag Tiger Cat II spindles, that bottom bolt is usually:

**15/16" (standard)** or **24mm (metric)**  

If yours looks a hair over 7/8", it’s 15/16".

### **2. Options if you don’t have that socket**

#### **A. Use an adjustable wrench (works, but not great)**
A quality adjustable can grab the bolt, but you need:

• The spindle locked from spinning  
• A tight grip  
• Slow, steady force  

It might round the edges if the bolt is frozen on there, so be mindful.

#### **B. Use a big crescent wrench with a pipe for leverage**
A long handle gives you the torque you need.  
Still: lock the spindle at the top pulley!

#### **C. Borrow or buy just the single socket**
You don’t need a whole kit. One 15/16" or 24mm deep socket will run like $4–6 at Harbor Freight, Walmart, etc.  
A cheap one works fine for this job.

#### **D. Use a box-end wrench (if one fits)**
A 15/16" box-end is actually *fantastic* because it won’t slip like an adjustable.

#### **E. Lock the bolt in vice grips (last resort)**
Clamp them on *tight* and use a hammer to tap the grips to break it loose.  
Not ideal, but it can work if you’re careful.
