### 📋 **What these codes typically mean:**

- **Fuel System Too Rich (P0153/P0154):**  
  This means the ECU is detecting more fuel than air in the combustion mix. Causes can include:
  - Faulty fuel injectors (leaking or stuck open)
  - Faulty fuel pressure regulator
  - Dirty or faulty Mass Air Flow (MAF) sensor (if applicable)
  - Faulty Oxygen (O2) sensor
  - Clogged air filter
  - Leaking fuel line or fuel return system
  - ECM/PCM problems

- **HO2S Voltage High (P0161, P0162):**  
  This refers to the **post-cat O2 sensors** (Bank 1 Sensor 2 and Bank 2 Sensor 2) showing consistently high voltage, which typically means:
  - O2 sensor is faulty
  - Fuel system is indeed running rich
  - Exhaust leaks before the sensor
  - Wiring or ground issue to O2 sensors

---

### 🚘 **What You Should Do Next**

1. **Check the air intake system:**
   - Clean or replace the **air filter**
   - Check for any vacuum leaks
   - If your car has a MAF sensor, clean it carefully

2. **Inspect the fuel system:**
   - Test or replace **fuel injectors** if they are leaking
   - Check **fuel pressure regulator** for proper function

3. **Check Oxygen sensors:**
   - Since you're getting high voltage readings from the post-cat sensors, it might be time to replace them
   - Check wiring and connectors to make sure they aren’t damaged or corroded

4. **Look for any exhaust leaks** before the oxygen sensors — leaks can throw off sensor readings.

5. **Scan live data (if available):**
   - Look at long-term and short-term fuel trims (LTFT, STFT). If they're significantly negative, it confirms a rich condition.

## ✅ **DIAGNOSTIC CHECKLIST (Step-by-Step)**

### 🔧 Step 1: **Scan Live Fuel Trim Data**
- [ ] Use an OBD2 scanner with live data.
- [ ] Check **Short-Term Fuel Trim (STFT)** and **Long-Term Fuel Trim (LTFT)** for both banks.
  - **Below -10%** = engine running rich.
- [ ] Check live readings of O2 sensors (B1S2 and B2S2).
  - Constantly high voltage (above 0.8V) = rich condition or sensor issue.

---

### 🌀 Step 2: **Inspect Air Intake System**
- [ ] Check **air filter** (replace if dirty)
- [ ] Look for **vacuum leaks** (cracked hoses, intake boot, PCV valve)
- [ ] If equipped, **clean the Mass Air Flow (MAF) sensor** with MAF-safe cleaner

---

### 💦 Step 3: **Fuel System Inspection**
- [ ] Test **fuel pressure** with gauge (too high = rich)
- [ ] Inspect **fuel pressure regulator** (may be vacuum-operated)
- [ ] Check **fuel injectors** for:
  - Leaking/stuck open
  - Excessive fuel delivery
- [ ] Look for leaking or pinched **fuel return line**

---

### 🔥 Step 4: **Oxygen Sensors**
- [ ] Replace or test **Bank 1 Sensor 2 (B1S2)** – post-cat sensor
- [ ] Replace or test **Bank 2 Sensor 2 (B2S2)** – post-cat sensor
- [ ] Inspect **wiring and connectors** to the sensors for damage/corrosion

---

### 💨 Step 5: **Exhaust Inspection**
- [ ] Check for **exhaust leaks** before the O2 sensors — especially near the manifold or downpipe
- [ ] Check catalytic converter condition if O2 sensors were recently replaced but codes persist

---

### 🧠 Step 6: **ECU & Sensor Review**
- [ ] Ensure **Engine Coolant Temperature (ECT) sensor** is reading correctly — if it reads cold all the time, it can cause a rich mix
- [ ] Verify no software updates or recalls affecting fuel trims (rare but possible)
