**Code P0420** on a **Honda Accord** means:

> **"Catalyst System Efficiency Below Threshold (Bank 1)"**

### ✅ What it Means:
This code indicates that your **catalytic converter** on **Bank 1** (the side with cylinder #1) is not operating as efficiently as it should. The **oxygen sensors before and after the catalytic converter** are reading too similarly, suggesting the cat isn’t doing its job of cleaning up exhaust gases.

---

### 🔍 Common Causes:
1. **Failing Catalytic Converter** (most common)
2. **Faulty Rear O2 Sensor (Downstream)**
3. **Exhaust Leak** near the manifold or converter
4. **Engine misfires, poor fuel mixture**
5. **Worn spark plugs or ignition issues**
6. **Bad upstream O2 sensor** (less common)
7. **Leaky fuel injectors or faulty fuel pressure regulator**

---

### 🧰 How to Diagnose:
1. **Check for exhaust leaks** – especially before the catalytic converter.
2. **Monitor O2 sensor readings** (Upstream vs Downstream) with an OBD2 scanner:
   - If the rear sensor mimics the front one, the cat is likely bad.
3. **Check long-term fuel trims (LTFT)** for lean/rich running.
4. **Inspect spark plugs** and check for signs of fouling or misfire.

---

### 🔧 Possible Fixes:
| Fix | Approximate Cost |
|-----|------------------|
| Replace catalytic converter | $400–$1,200+ |
| Replace downstream O2 sensor | $60–$150 (DIY) |
| Fix exhaust leaks | $50–$300 |

| Replace upstream O2 sensor | $60–$150 |

### 🚗 2003–2007 Honda Accord Note:
These models are known to **throw false P0420 codes** due to aging O2 sensors even when the cat is still working. So try replacing the **rear O2 sensor first** before replacing the catalytic converter.

To test the **downstream O2 sensor** (also called the **post-cat oxygen sensor**) on your **Honda Accord**, follow these steps. This sensor tells the ECU how well the **catalytic converter** is cleaning up the exhaust gases.

---

## 🔧 Tools You’ll Need:
- **OBD2 scanner** (ideally one that shows live data)
- **Digital multimeter (DMM)** (optional, for more manual testing)
- **Basic tools** (to access the sensor if needed)

---

## ✅ Method 1: Using a Scan Tool (Easiest + Most Accurate)

### 🔍 What to Look For:
- Plug in the OBD2 scanner and look at **live data** from:
  - **O2 Sensor 1 (Upstream)** — before the catalytic converter
  - **O2 Sensor 2 (Downstream)** — after the catalytic converter

### 🔄 Interpreting the Readings:
- **Upstream O2 (Sensor 1)**: Should **bounce** between **0.1V – 0.9V** rapidly (normal).
- **Downstream O2 (Sensor 2)**:
  - Should be **much more stable**, ideally around **0.45V**.
  - If it **mimics the upstream sensor** (bouncing a lot), the **catalytic converter is not working** or the **sensor is faulty**.
  - If it’s stuck at **0V** or **>0.9V**, the sensor might be dead.

---

## 🔌 Method 2: Using a Multimeter (Manual Test)

> You can test voltage output directly from the sensor while the engine is warm and idling.

### Steps:
1. **Locate the sensor** (downstream sensor is after the catalytic converter, usually in the exhaust pipe).
2. **Back-probe the signal wire** (usually the black wire).
3. **Set the multimeter to DC volts (0–2V)**.
4. **Start the engine** and monitor the voltage.

### Good Readings:
- A **working downstream sensor** should hold steady near **0.45V**.
- If it shows no voltage, or jumps erratically like the upstream, it may be **faulty or reading false data**.

---

## 🔥 Heat Test (Basic Check):
The downstream sensor only works **after warming up** (closed loop mode). Let the car idle for **5–10 minutes**. If you tap the exhaust near the sensor and the reading changes erratically, that may indicate it's sensitive or failing.

---

## 🛠 When to Replace It:
- Reading is **flat-lined** or stuck high/low
- Matches upstream sensor readings too closely
- Throws code **P0138**, **P0141**, or **P0420**
- Aged sensor (100k+ miles)
