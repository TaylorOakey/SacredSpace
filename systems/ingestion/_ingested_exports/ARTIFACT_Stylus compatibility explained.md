### ⚙ **ASSISTANT** — 2025-11-14 23:40

A stylus is basically a conversation between your finger and the touchscreen, and every phone has its own “accent.”  

Your Moto G Power’s touchscreen speaks **capacitive**—it listens for changes in electrical charge.  
The Samsung Note stylus (the S-Pen) speaks **Wacom EMR**—a totally different language that uses tiny electromagnetic pulses from a special sensor hidden *behind* the Note’s screen.

The result is a bit like trying to use a violin bow on a guitar. Both are instruments, both have strings, but the mechanics don’t line up.

Here’s the quick breakdown, plain and true:

### The S-Pen will not work on the Moto G Power.
Not because it’s old, but because the Moto G Power doesn’t have the hardware layer that reads the S-Pen’s electromagnetic signals.

But the universe isn’t closed here—there *are* stylus options.

### What *will* work on your Moto G Power:
1. **Any capacitive stylus.**  
   These imitate your fingertip. They’re cheap, universal, and simple.  
   No pressure sensitivity, but great for tapping, scrolling, light sketching.

2. **Active Bluetooth styluses that emulate pressure.**  
   Brands like Adonit, Logitech Crayon (for some Androids), and others.  
   These don’t use Wacom tech, so your phone can understand them.

3. **Drawing tablets that pair to your phone.**  
   Wacom Intuos, Huion Inspiroy, XP-Pen Deco.  
   This gives you real pressure sensitivity and feels closer to a Note experience—but the stylus interacts with the tablet, not the screen.
