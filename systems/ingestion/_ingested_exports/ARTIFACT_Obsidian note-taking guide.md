## **Obsidian Basics**

Obsidian is a note-taking environment that stores everything locally in “vaults” — simple folders filled with Markdown (.md) files. Markdown is lightweight formatting for the web: **bold**, *italics*, links, images, and more. Obsidian’s **Live Preview** mode hides the raw symbols and presents notes almost as finished pages while you type.

The charm here is freedom: your notes aren’t trapped in an app. They’re plain text — portable, durable, future-proof.

---

## **Linking and Daily Notes**

Daily Notes act like anchors in time, catching the passing sparks of thought. They’re excellent for fleeting ideas, quick logs, or morning reflections.

Links (`[[Like This]]`) bind your ideas together into a living network. Each link automatically generates a **backlink**, revealing everywhere that idea is mentioned across your vault. This creates an emergent web of meaning.

The **Graph View** renders this network visually — a kind of brain-constellation map where clusters reveal themes, relationships, or even blind spots waiting for discovery.

---

## **Headings and Embedding**

Headings (written as `#`, `##`, etc.) structure your notes into clear sections. Obsidian lets you reference and embed specific sections from *other* notes using:

- `![[Note Name#Heading]]` for sections  
- `![[Note Name^blockID]]` for precise blocks  

This unlocks a modular style of writing where you build once, reuse everywhere. Split-pane view lets you keep several notes open side-by-side — a researcher's playground.

---

## **Hashtags and Searching**

Hashtags act as broad classifiers (`#philosophy`, `#project`, `#todo`). They’re great for themes that don’t yet deserve their own note.

Links = specific concept pages  
Tags = broad searchable topics

Obsidian’s search engine is surprisingly powerful, using operators like:

- `AND`  
- `OR`  
- `-` (exclude)  
- `""` (exact phrase)  
- Regex (advanced pattern matching)

Search becomes a kind of spelunking: you dive into your vault and rediscover buried threads.
